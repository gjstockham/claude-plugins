# Pantheon design reference

Sources: Inkwell Ideas Fantasy Religion Design Guide; Red Ragged Fiend "Making Gods & Pantheons" (1d4 domains method); MTG color pie as a values scaffold; Step into RPGs on avoiding one-god-per-domain.

## Purpose of religion (ground each god in one)
Religions exist to (1) explain the unexplainable (why droughts, why death, why the harvest), (2) provide hope and purpose, (3) enforce a social order, (4) mark time and transition (birth/marriage/death, seasons). Every god should answer a real question the worshippers have — derived from their subsistence, environment, and history.

## Domains (roll or pick 1d4 per god; domains may be shared)
Sky, Sun, Moon(s), Storm, Sea, Rivers & Fresh Water, Earth & Mountains, Fire, Harvest & Grain, Fertility, Beasts & the Hunt, Forests, Death & the Dead, War & Conquest, Craft & Smithing, Trade & Wealth, Travel & Roads, Law & Oaths, Kingship & Order, Chaos & Trickery, Love & Desire, Knowledge & Secrets, Healing & Plague, Fate & Time, Ancestors, Justice & Vengeance, Madness & Prophecy, Winter & Cold, Luck.

Method: give each god 1d4 domains. Let domains overlap (two gods can both touch War; a god can hold Death + Rivers + Ancestors). Overlap creates rival cults and theological disputes — good for intrigue.

## Values scaffold (MTG color pie, optional)
Assign each god a value-axis so the pantheon expresses a coherent set of tensions rather than random portfolios:
- **Order** (structure, law, sacrifice-for-the-whole) vs **Freedom** (individual, change, cunning).
- **Might** (strength, growth, instinct) vs **Mind** (knowledge, control, artifice).
- **Community/Peace** vs **Ambition/Conflict**.
A pantheon reads as a system when its gods sit at different points on these axes and their `relations` reflect the tensions.

## Placement logic (drives patron_of & temples)
- Match portfolio to worshippers: coastal/seafaring → sea, storm, travel; farming → harvest, fertility, sun, rivers; pastoral/steppe → sky, beasts, war, ancestors; mining/mountain → earth, craft, fire; desert/caravan → travel, trade, water, death; northern/taiga → winter, the hunt, ancestors.
- Historical trauma breeds gods: a people shaped by a great drought/collapse may venerate a god of endurance, water, or the dead.
- Temples cluster in capitals and in the region most associated with the god (a river god's great temple at the river-crossing capital).

## Anti-clichés
- Not one god per domain — share and overlap.
- Not a tidy nine-god grid of alignments — let it be lopsided, with dominant and minor cults.
- Not universally benevolent — gods can be feared, appeased, or bargained with, matching tone.
